How Much Does a Wedding Usually Cost in Italy?

Understand typical wedding costs in Italy, what changes the budget, and how to build a realistic destination wedding budget without relying on misleading averages.

9 min read  ·  WeddingCoding Editorial

There is no single price for a wedding in Italy. A small civil ceremony followed by dinner for 20 guests can cost a fraction of a three-day celebration at a luxury villa. The most useful budget starts with your guest count, location, season and the experience you want to create.

The short answer: what should you budget?

As a broad starting point, many conventional Italian weddings are planned in the tens of thousands of euros. An industry report on weddings held in 2024 put the average at approximately €23,781, while destination weddings organised by couples living abroad can sit in a different price bracket because they often include venue exclusivity, travel logistics and several events. The average is context, not a quote: it may not describe your wedding at all.

Why published averages differ

Reports measure different populations. Some cover Italian resident couples; others focus on foreign couples travelling to Italy. Some count the wedding day only, while others include welcome dinners, accommodation or guest experiences. Sample size, region, guest count and luxury level also change the result. Always check what a statistic includes before using it to set your own budget.

The biggest cost drivers

Guest count is often the strongest variable because catering, drinks, rentals, favours and sometimes transport scale with each additional guest. Venue hire and supplier fees may be fixed or tiered. Other important variables include the day of the week, peak-season demand, the venue’s exclusivity, travel and accommodation, floral complexity, photography and video hours, entertainment, and whether the celebration lasts one day or several.

A practical budget checklist

Start with venue and ceremony costs; food and drinks; planner and coordination; photography and video; flowers and styling; music and entertainment; stationery and guest communications; transport and accommodation; attire and rings; legal or celebrant fees; and a contingency reserve. Clarify whether quotes include VAT, service, setup, breakdown, overtime, travel and backup plans for bad weather.

Three planning scenarios

A small, simple celebration may keep costs lower by limiting guest count, choosing a weekday or shoulder season, and hosting one main event. A mid-size wedding can balance a distinctive venue with selective styling and a clear supplier brief. A multi-day destination wedding with exclusive-use accommodation, premium catering and extensive production requires a substantially larger budget. These are planning profiles, not fixed price bands: ask suppliers for quotes based on your actual brief.

How to compare supplier quotes fairly

Create one scope document and send it to each supplier. Compare deliverables rather than headline prices: hours, staff, travel, equipment, taxes, cancellation terms, payment dates, and what happens if plans change. Keep estimates, confirmed contracts, deposits paid and outstanding balances separate. This makes it easier to spot budget drift before it becomes a problem.

Plan the guest experience, not just the ceremony

For destination weddings, guest information can affect the experience as much as décor. Clear travel instructions, an itinerary, FAQs, digital invitations and a mobile-friendly wedding website help guests feel prepared. Digital tools cannot reduce every cost, but they can make planning information easier to share and maintain.

A simple way to start

Set a maximum total budget, estimate the number of guests, list non-negotiables, then allocate provisional amounts to the main categories. Ask suppliers for real quotes and update the plan as decisions become final.
