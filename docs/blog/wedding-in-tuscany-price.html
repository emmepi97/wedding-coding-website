Wedding in Tuscany Price: What Should You Budget?

Understand the price of a wedding in Tuscany, typical budget categories, cost variables and ways to compare supplier quotes fairly.

WeddingCoding Editorial · Practical guide

There is no single price for a wedding in Tuscany. An intimate ceremony, a one-day celebration for local guests and a multi-day destination wedding at an exclusive villa have very different costs. The useful question is not “What is the price?” but “What is included in the price for the experience we want?”

Use ranges as planning tools, not promises

Recent 2026 planning guides commonly place a wedding for roughly 60–80 guests in Tuscany in the tens of thousands of euros, with many full-service estimates landing around €50,000–€100,000 and premium multi-day events significantly higher. One published model for 70 guests gives a broad €48,000–€130,000 range; the figures are estimates, not supplier quotes. See the Tuscany budget model and check every assumption before relying on a headline number.

Know where the money goes

The venue can be a major fixed cost; catering and drinks usually scale with guest count. Other categories include planning, photography and film, flowers and styling, music, rentals, ceremony costs, transport, accommodation, stationery, beauty services, taxes and contingency. If the celebration spans several days, food, staffing, transport and coverage can grow quickly.

Guest count is one of the biggest levers

Each additional guest may affect catering, drinks, furniture, staffing, transport and sometimes the size or price tier of the venue. If the budget feels too high, compare a smaller guest list, a weekday date, a single-day format, a less complex floral design or a venue with suitable furniture already included.

Compare total costs, not the starting price

A venue quote might exclude catering, furniture, cleaning, security, accommodation, sound equipment or a weather backup. Supplier quotes may exclude VAT, travel, overtime, extra staff or delivery. Ask for an itemised proposal and a clear list of inclusions and exclusions. Put every quote into the same comparison format.

Protect the budget with a contingency

Set aside a reserve for unexpected costs. The appropriate amount depends on how many details are still undecided and how weather-sensitive the event is. Keep the contingency visible rather than quietly spending it on upgrades. Record estimates, confirmed costs, deposits paid, upcoming deadlines and remaining balances.

Spend where it matters most to you

Prioritise the elements guests will experience most or that carry personal meaning. For some couples, that is food and music; for others, photography, accommodation or an intimate venue. Avoid assuming that a higher price automatically means a better fit. Clear communication and a coherent plan are often more valuable than adding more elements.

Make budget management simpler

A shared digital tracker can help you see the full cost, compare supplier quotes and remember due dates. Use it alongside signed contracts and supplier invoices, which remain the authoritative records.
