Destination Wedding in Italy: A Month-by-Month Planning Checklist

A month-by-month checklist for organising an Italian destination wedding, from budget and venue research to guest travel and final confirmations.

WeddingCoding Editorial · Practical guide

Planning a wedding in another country can feel like a long chain of decisions. A simple timeline helps you focus on what matters now, while leaving room for local requirements, supplier availability and changes in your plans. Use this as a flexible framework, not a rigid rulebook.

12–18 months before: define the foundations

Agree on a working budget, guest-count range, preferred regions and overall style. Research ceremony requirements based on your nationality and circumstances. Shortlist venues, ask for date availability and compare what is included. If you want a planner, interview candidates before committing to a venue if you need their local expertise.

9–12 months before: book the core team

Confirm the venue and the main suppliers whose availability matters most: planner, photographer, catering, music and ceremony professionals. Review contracts carefully and record deposit amounts, due dates, cancellation terms and inclusions. Decide whether the celebration includes a welcome event or farewell gathering.

6–9 months before: design the guest experience

Create a wedding website with travel advice, accommodation options, schedule expectations and FAQs. Send save-the-dates or invitations at an appropriate point for your guest list and destination. Discuss food, accessibility, transport and any language needs with suppliers.

3–6 months before: refine the details

Confirm the menu, drinks, music, flowers, rentals, ceremony format and photography timeline. Track RSVPs and dietary requirements. Check passport or travel-document guidance with the relevant authorities where appropriate; do not assume every guest has the same requirements. Review legal paperwork with the municipality or consulate if you are marrying legally in Italy.

1–3 months before: confirm every moving part

Update the guest list, rooming information and transport plan. Confirm supplier arrival times, contact numbers, setup windows, balances due and rain-plan decisions. Share the latest schedule with the people who need it. Keep a contingency budget for weather, transport or last-minute changes.

The final two weeks: reduce uncertainty

Reconfirm all vendors, guest numbers, dietary notes, transfers and ceremony timings. Prepare a short contact sheet and designate a trusted point person for urgent questions. Avoid making unnecessary design changes at the last minute. Keep copies of essential documents accessible and secure.

After the wedding: close the loop

Settle remaining invoices according to contract terms, collect galleries and films, return rentals and thank guests and suppliers. Save key photos, messages and documents in an organised archive. If you want to preserve memories in a more personal way, WeddingCoding’s [time capsule demo](../demos/time-capsule/index.html) offers an idea for collecting messages for the future.

Use one source of truth

A budget tracker, a supplier directory, a current guest list and a shared schedule can prevent contradictory versions from circulating. Plan with more clarity.

Keep the checklist flexible, assign responsibility for each task and update one shared plan so everyone works from the same information.
