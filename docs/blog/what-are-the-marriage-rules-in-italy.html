What Are the Marriage Rules in Italy?

Understand the main legal conditions, paperwork, banns, witnesses and ceremony differences couples should verify before marrying in Italy.

9 min read  ·  WeddingCoding Editorial

Marriage in Italy follows national law, while some administrative steps are handled by the municipality and can vary according to the couple’s circumstances. Foreign couples should confirm their own requirements directly with the local civil-status office and the relevant consular authorities.

Legal capacity and consent

Both people must have the legal capacity to marry and must freely consent. Italian law sets conditions relating to age, existing marital status, close family relationships and other impediments. Exceptions and procedures can be specific, so do not use a general article to determine whether an individual case qualifies.

Minimum age

The general rule is that a person must be 18 to marry. Italian law provides a narrow judicial authorisation route for certain minors who are at least 16, subject to statutory conditions. Couples should not assume that parental consent alone is sufficient. Confirm any exceptional case with a qualified legal professional or the competent authority.

Existing marriage and civil status

A person who is already legally married generally cannot enter another marriage until the prior marriage has legally ended or the relevant legal status has been established. Divorce, annulment and widowhood can require official documents. Foreign documents may need translation or authentication.

Marriage banns and municipal procedures

Italian citizens marrying before Italian authorities are generally subject to the applicable marriage-publication procedure, with details depending on residence and circumstances. The municipality will explain whether banns, appointments or additional steps are required for your case. Foreign couples should ask the municipality how the procedure applies to them rather than assume the same rules as Italian residents.

Foreign nationals and the nulla osta

Foreign nationals generally need to present a certificate or declaration from the competent authority confirming there is no impediment to marriage, under the rules applicable to their nationality. Country-specific agreements may change the required document or formalities. Ask whether translation, legalisation or apostille is needed.

Civil, religious and symbolic ceremonies

A civil ceremony before a competent authority can create a legal marriage. Certain religious ceremonies may have civil effects when legal requirements are satisfied. A symbolic ceremony or private vow exchange does not automatically establish a legal marriage. Confirm the legal status of the exact ceremony you are booking.

Witnesses, language and venue

Confirm witness requirements, identification documents, whether an interpreter is necessary and whether the ceremony can be held at your chosen venue. Municipalities may have venue rules and fees. Ask for written confirmation of logistics and deadlines.

After the wedding

Request the official certificate and check whether you need a translation, apostille or registration with your home country. Recognition abroad is governed by the rules of the relevant country. Keep official documents secure and avoid publishing them on a public wedding website.

Your reliable checklist

Ask the municipality for a personalised list of documents; contact the consulate early; verify name spellings and validity periods; build time for translations and legalisation; confirm ceremony type and officiant; and keep a record of official answers. Important legal note

This overview is for general information only and is not a substitute for legal advice. The applicable law and documentation depend on individual circumstances and can change.
