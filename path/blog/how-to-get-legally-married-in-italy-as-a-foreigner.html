How to Get Legally Married in Italy as a Foreigner

A practical step-by-step overview for foreign couples planning a legally recognised marriage in Italy, including documents, translations, timing and municipal checks.

10 min read  ·  WeddingCoding Editorial

Foreign nationals can marry in Italy, but the paperwork depends on nationality, residence, previous marriages and the municipality where the ceremony will take place. Start with the civil-status office and your consulate before setting a final timetable or paying non-refundable suppliers.

Step 1: Decide which ceremony you need

First establish whether you want a legally binding civil marriage in Italy, a religious ceremony with civil effects where permitted, or a symbolic celebration. If you are legally marrying in another country and holding a symbolic ceremony in Italy, the document process may be very different.

Step 2: Contact the municipality where you plan to marry

Ask the Ufficio di Stato Civile which documents are required for both partners, the deadline for submitting them, whether an appointment is needed and what language or format is accepted. Municipal procedures can differ, so avoid relying on another couple’s checklist without verifying it.

Step 3: Ask your consulate about the certificate of no impediment

Under Article 116 of the Italian Civil Code, foreign citizens generally need to provide documentation from the competent authority of their country confirming there is no impediment to marriage. This is commonly referred to as a certificate of no impediment or nulla osta, but the exact document and process depend on nationality and applicable agreements.

Step 4: Check translations, legalisation and apostille requirements

Some documents may need an official Italian translation, legalisation or an apostille. There are exceptions and country-specific arrangements, so ask the municipality and issuing authority what applies to your documents. Confirm whether names, dates and place names match across passports and certificates.

Step 5: Prepare identity and civil-status documents

You will typically need valid identity documents and evidence relevant to your civil status. If either partner is divorced or widowed, additional records may be required. Do not assume the same supporting documents apply to every nationality or situation; request a personalised checklist from the municipality.

Step 6: Confirm witnesses, interpreter and ceremony logistics

Ask how many witnesses are required, whether they need identification, and whether an interpreter is necessary if one of the parties does not understand Italian. Confirm the venue, time, officiant and any rules for ceremonies outside the town hall.

Step 7: Plan the timetable with a buffer

Allow time for consular appointments, document issuance, translation and legalisation. Some documents have validity periods or processing delays. Build a buffer and avoid booking a tight sequence that depends on every administrative step happening on the earliest possible date.

Step 8: Obtain and register the marriage certificate

After the ceremony, ask how and when the official certificate or extract will be issued, whether multilingual forms are available and what your home country requires for recognition or registration. Keep secure copies of all official records.

A planning checklist

Create one folder for each partner, keep a checklist with owner and deadline for every document, and record the name of the official who confirmed each requirement. Your wedding website and guest chatbot should handle guest-facing information such as travel and schedule; keep passports, certificates and private legal details off public pages.

Important legal note

This is a general planning guide, not legal advice. Rules can change and individual circumstances matter. The municipality and your consulate are the authoritative contacts for your case.
