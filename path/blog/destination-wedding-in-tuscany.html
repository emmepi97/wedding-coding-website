Destination Wedding in Tuscany: A Complete Planning Guide

Plan a destination wedding in Tuscany with advice on regions, venues, season, guest travel, budget and digital organisation.

WeddingCoding Editorial · Practical guide

A destination wedding in Tuscany can mean a vineyard dinner among the hills, a historic villa near Florence, a medieval village celebration or a relaxed countryside weekend. The region’s variety is its greatest strength—and the reason it pays to define the experience before choosing a venue.

Choose the Tuscany that feels like you

Chianti is known for vineyard landscapes and country estates; Val d’Orcia offers iconic rolling hills and cypress-lined roads; Florence combines art, architecture and convenient city access; Siena and its surroundings bring medieval character; the coast and Elba offer a different, sea-oriented atmosphere. Think about the experience you want guests to have between events, not only the ceremony backdrop.

Set the guest experience before the venue search

Estimate the number of guests, how many need accommodation, whether you want a welcome dinner and farewell brunch, and how much transport you can provide. A remote villa may be unforgettable but requires a plan for airport transfers, taxis, late-night returns and accessibility. Compare the full logistics of each location.

Choose the season with practicalities in mind

Late spring and early autumn are popular for comfortable temperatures and outdoor scenery, but demand can be high. Summer brings long evenings and lush event schedules, while heat, shade and hydration need attention. Spring and autumn dates can offer a distinct atmosphere, but always plan an indoor or covered alternative for ceremony and dinner.

Understand the budget drivers

The venue, catering and drinks, accommodation, planner, photography, flowers, music and guest transport are usually the major categories. A recent ENIT report on the 2025 destination-wedding market described Tuscany as a leading region, with an average event budget around €74,000; this is an industry-wide benchmark and not a minimum or a quote for every couple. Read the ENIT destination-wedding report and build your own budget around your guest count and priorities.

Book the key suppliers in the right order

After setting a working budget and approximate guest count, shortlist venues and check availability. Then confirm the planner or coordinator, catering approach, photographer, music and any legal ceremony requirements. Ask each supplier what is included, whether VAT and travel are extra, and how payment milestones work.

Make the wedding weekend easy to navigate

Guests travelling internationally need clear information: airports, train options, driving times, accommodation blocks, dress code, weather expectations and the event schedule. A dedicated wedding website helps keep updates in one place. Digital invitations and an FAQ can reduce repeated questions and make the experience feel thoughtful before guests arrive.

Use digital tools as the invisible support system

A budget tracker, shared planning dashboard, digital invitation flow and guest chatbot can reduce friction without taking attention away from the celebration.
